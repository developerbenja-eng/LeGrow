## What Plants Need

Growing strawberries indoors under artificial light requires understanding a few key metrics that determine whether your plants will thrive, merely survive, or fail entirely.

### PPFD (Photosynthetic Photon Flux Density)

PPFD measures the number of photosynthetically active photons that hit a square meter of canopy every second, expressed in micromoles per square meter per second (umol/m^2/s).

- **Strawberry target:** 300-350 umol/m^2/s
- **For reference:** Cannabis targets 800-1000 umol/m^2/s -- roughly double to triple what strawberries need
- **Seedling/clone stage:** 150-200 umol/m^2/s
- **Vegetative growth:** 200-300 umol/m^2/s
- **Flowering and fruiting:** 300-350 umol/m^2/s

This is a major advantage for berry growers: you need significantly less light intensity than the cannabis cultivators who drive most of the grow light market. That means lower wattage, lower electricity bills, and less heat to manage.

### DLI (Daily Light Integral)

DLI is the total amount of photosynthetically active light delivered to a plant over a 24-hour period, measured in moles per square meter per day (mol/m^2/day). Think of PPFD as the "speed" of light delivery and DLI as the "total distance traveled" in a day.

- **Strawberry target for fruiting:** 17-22 mol/m^2/day
- **Formula:** DLI = PPFD x hours x 0.0036
- **Example:** 300 umol/m^2/s x 16 hours x 0.0036 = 17.3 mol/m^2/day
- **Example:** 350 umol/m^2/s x 15 hours x 0.0036 = 18.9 mol/m^2/day

### Photoperiod

Strawberries are facultative long-day plants. Most everbearing and day-neutral varieties fruit best with long photoperiods.

- **Recommended:** 14-16 hours on, 6-8 hours rest (dark period)
- **Dark period is essential:** Plants perform critical metabolic processes during the dark period, including starch-to-sugar conversion that affects fruit sweetness
- **Consistency matters:** Use a timer and keep the schedule rock-solid; irregular photoperiods stress the plant and can delay or reduce fruiting

### Spectrum

Modern full-spectrum white LEDs provide a good baseline, but understanding the specific wavelengths that drive strawberry performance allows you to choose the right fixture.

| Wavelength Range | Color | Role in Strawberry Growth | Impact |
|------------------|-------|---------------------------|--------|
| 400-500nm | Blue | Compact growth, strong stems, leaf development | Improves antioxidant content 15-20% |
| 500-600nm | Green | Canopy penetration, reaches lower leaves | Moderate contribution to photosynthesis |
| 600-700nm | Red | Drives photosynthesis, fruiting, flowering | Increases yield 20-30%, enhances sweetness |
| 700-750nm | Far-red | Phytochrome response, shade avoidance | Enhances leaf expansion and fruit production |

**Optimal red-to-blue ratio:** 3:1 to 4:1

Most quality full-spectrum LED grow lights already deliver close to this ratio. The key takeaway is that red light is the primary driver of fruiting and sweetness, while blue keeps the plant compact and boosts nutritional quality. Far-red is a bonus -- some higher-end fixtures include dedicated far-red diodes.

## LED Chip Technology (2026)

The LED chips (diodes) inside a grow light are the single most important factor in its performance. Higher-efficacy chips convert more electricity into usable plant light and waste less as heat.

### Samsung LM301H EVO (Current Best)

The Samsung LM301H EVO is the gold standard for grow light diodes as of 2024-2026.

- **Diode efficacy:** 3.14 umol/J (micromoles of photons per joule of electricity)
- **Full fixture efficacy:** 2.8-2.9 umol/J (after driver losses and thermal derating)
- **Blue peak:** Shifted to 437nm, optimized specifically for plant photosynthesis compared to the older 450nm peak
- **Thermal performance:** Lower heat output than all predecessors, meaning less cooling needed and longer diode lifespan
- **Availability:** Now found in mid-range fixtures, not just premium models

### Chip Comparison Table

| Chip | Diode Efficacy | Typical Fixture Efficacy | Era | Notes |
|------|---------------|------------------------|-----|-------|
| Samsung LM301B | 2.7 umol/J | 2.3-2.5 umol/J | 2019 | Still common in budget lights; proven and reliable |
| Samsung LM301H | 2.9 umol/J | 2.5-2.7 umol/J | 2021 | Added horticultural-optimized phosphor coating |
| **Samsung LM301H EVO** | **3.14 umol/J** | **2.8-2.9 umol/J** | **2024+** | **Current best; optimized blue peak at 437nm** |
| Bridgelux | 2.8 umol/J | 2.5-2.7 umol/J | 2023+ | Good budget alternative; used by Mars Hydro FC-E series |

### What Efficacy Means in Practice

Every 0.1 umol/J improvement in efficacy means roughly 3-5% less electricity for the same light output. Over the 50,000-hour lifespan of an LED fixture, this adds up. For a 300W light running 15 hours/day:

- At 2.5 umol/J: ~$9.00/month electricity (Memphis rates)
- At 2.8 umol/J: ~$8.00/month electricity
- At 3.0 umol/J: ~$7.50/month electricity

The savings are modest at Memphis electricity rates, but the higher-efficacy chips also produce less heat, which is the more meaningful practical benefit for an indoor grow.

## Light Fixture Styles

How the LED chips are arranged on the fixture matters almost as much as the chips themselves, especially for strawberries.

### Bar Style (Recommended for Strawberries)

Bar-style lights use multiple long, thin aluminum bars spread across a frame, each lined with LED diodes.

**Advantages:**
- **Even light distribution** across the entire canopy -- no hot spots, no dark corners
- **Runs cooler** because heat is spread across a large surface area of aluminum bars
- **Can hang closer to plants** (12-18 inches) without burning leaf tips
- **Ideal for low, wide plants like strawberries** that grow horizontally rather than vertically
- **Better canopy penetration** from multiple light angles

**Disadvantages:**
- Slightly more expensive than board-style at the same wattage
- More complex construction (more potential points of failure, though this is rare)
- Larger physical footprint

### Board Style (Quantum Board)

Board-style lights concentrate all LEDs on a single flat panel (the "quantum board").

**Advantages:**
- **Lower cost** -- simpler construction means cheaper manufacturing
- **Proven reliability** -- the most popular style for years, with extensive user track records
- **Compact form factor** -- easier to handle and store

**Disadvantages:**
- **Hot center, weaker edges** -- the center of the board gets significantly more light than the perimeter
- **Needs more hanging distance** (24-30 inches) to allow light to spread, which is a problem in short tents
- **Higher heat concentration** at the center of the board
- **Less ideal for strawberries** because their low, spreading growth habit means edge plants get noticeably less light

### Verdict for Strawberries

**Bar style wins.** Strawberries grow low and wide. They produce runners and spread horizontally. Even light distribution is more important for strawberries than for tall, single-cola plants. The ability to hang the light closer also helps in smaller tents where vertical space is limited by the plants' preferred temperature range.

## Strawberry-Specific Advantage

This is the single most important economic insight for berry growers using lights designed for the cannabis market.

### Dimming for Savings

Since strawberries need approximately 300 PPFD compared to cannabis at 800-1000 PPFD, you can run your grow light at **50-60% power** and still provide everything your plants need. This has cascading benefits:

| Factor | At 100% (Cannabis) | At 60% (Strawberries) | Savings |
|--------|--------------------|-----------------------|---------|
| Power draw (300W light) | 300W | ~180W | 40% less electricity |
| Heat output | ~300W thermal equiv. | ~180W thermal equiv. | 40% less cooling needed |
| LED stress | Higher current per diode | Lower current per diode | Longer diode lifespan |
| Electricity cost/month (Memphis) | ~$13.50 | ~$8.10 | ~$5.40/month saved |

### The Math

A 300W light dimmed to 60%:

- **Actual power draw:** ~180W
- **Daily consumption:** 180W x 15 hours = 2.7 kWh
- **Monthly consumption:** 2.7 kWh x 30 days = 81 kWh
- **Monthly cost at $0.10/kWh:** $8.10

### Bonus: Longer LED Life at Reduced Power

LEDs degrade faster at higher temperatures and higher drive currents. Running at 60% keeps the junction temperature lower and the current per diode lower, which can extend the effective lifespan from 50,000 hours to 60,000+ hours. At 15 hours/day, that is the difference between ~9 years and ~11 years.

## Heat Management

Heat is the enemy of strawberry flavor and fruit set. Understanding and managing the thermal output of your grow light is critical.

### How Much Heat Does a Dimmed LED Produce?

An LED grow light converts roughly 40-45% of input electricity to photosynthetically active light, with the remainder becoming heat. At 60% power:

- **A 300W light at 60%** draws ~180W and produces approximately 100-110W of heat
- **For perspective:** That is roughly the heat output of a laptop and a half, or a single incandescent light bulb
- This is a very manageable amount of heat

### Strawberry Temperature Preferences

| Parameter | Target Range | Danger Zone |
|-----------|-------------|-------------|
| Daytime temperature | 68-75 degrees F (20-24 degrees C) | Above 85 degrees F: fruit set drops dramatically |
| Nighttime temperature | 50-54 degrees F (10-12 degrees C) | Below 28 degrees F: crown damage |
| Temperature differential (day/night) | 15-20 degrees F swing | Less than 10 degrees F swing: reduced sweetness |

The day/night temperature differential is important for strawberry sweetness. Cool nights allow the plant to accumulate sugars rather than burning them through respiration.

### Cooling Strategy

For most indoor strawberry grows, the cooling strategy is straightforward:

1. **Inline exhaust fan:** A 4-inch or 6-inch inline fan (like an AC Infinity Cloudline) is sufficient to remove the heat from a dimmed LED and maintain target temperatures
2. **No AC typically needed:** Unlike cannabis grows running 600W+ at full power, a strawberry grow at 180W actual draw produces very little heat
3. **Passive intake:** A tent's passive intake vents are usually sufficient when paired with an inline exhaust

### Pro Tip: Run Lights During Off-Peak / Nighttime Hours

**Schedule your lights to run from 6:00 PM to 8:00 AM (or similar nighttime schedule).**

Benefits of a nighttime light schedule:

- **Naturally cooler ambient air** drawn in through intake during the hottest part of the light cycle
- **"Night" (lights off) occurs during the warmest part of the day**, making it easier to hit the 50-54 degree F target naturally in cooler months
- **Lower electricity rates** in some utility plans (though MLGW residential does not currently have time-of-use pricing)
- **Daytime rest period** means you can access and work on the tent during the day with lights off

### Heat Summary by Setup

| Setup | Heat Output | Cooling Required |
|-------|------------|-----------------|
| 2x4, 130W actual | ~75W heat | 4-inch inline fan |
| 3x3, 200W actual | ~115W heat | 4-inch or 6-inch inline fan |
| 4x4, 300W actual | ~170W heat | 6-inch inline fan |

## Comparison: Strawberry vs Cannabis Light Needs

This comparison is useful because almost all grow light reviews, guides, and PAR maps are written for cannabis cultivation. Understanding the differences helps you interpret cannabis-focused resources for strawberry growing.

| Parameter | Strawberry | Cannabis | Implication for Berry Growers |
|-----------|-----------|---------|-------------------------------|
| Light hours (photoperiod) | 14-16 hrs on | 18/6 (veg) or 12/12 (flower) | Moderate photoperiod; similar electricity use |
| Daytime temperature | 68-75 degrees F | 70-80 degrees F | Strawberries like it slightly cooler |
| Nighttime temperature | 50-54 degrees F | 60-70 degrees F | Much cooler nights needed; helps sweetness |
| Humidity (RH) | 40-60% | 40-60% | Similar requirements |
| Wattage per sq ft | 30-40W | 30-50W | Similar at fixture level, but dimmed lower |
| PPFD target | 300-350 umol/m^2/s | 800-1000 umol/m^2/s | Dim to 50-60%; major electricity savings |
| DLI target | 17-22 mol/m^2/day | 40-60 mol/m^2/day | Much lower total light needed |
| CO2 supplementation | Not needed | Common at high PPFD | Saves money and complexity |
| Light style preference | Bar (even spread) | Bar or board | Low canopy makes even spread critical |
| Spectrum priority | High red for fruiting | High red for flowering | Same spectrum works for both |

### Key Takeaway

**A grow light marketed as "good for a 3x3 cannabis flower" is overkill for a 3x3 strawberry grow -- and that is an advantage.** You buy the light, dim it to 60%, run it at lower temperatures, use less electricity, and the diodes last longer. The cannabis market's scale drives down prices on lights that are more than capable for berry growing.

## Light Lifespan

### LED Longevity

Quality LED grow lights are rated for extremely long lifespans:

- **Rated lifespan:** 50,000 hours (to 70% of original output, known as L70)
- **At 15 hours/day:** 50,000 / 15 = 3,333 days = **~9.1 years**
- **At 60% dim (lower thermal stress):** Realistically 55,000-65,000 hours = **~10-12 years**

### Degradation Pattern

LEDs do not burn out like incandescent bulbs. They gradually lose output:

| Age | Expected Output (at rated drive) | Expected Output (at 60% drive) |
|-----|--------------------------------|-------------------------------|
| Year 0 | 100% | 100% |
| Year 3 | ~95% | ~97% |
| Year 5 | ~90% | ~94% |
| Year 7 | ~85% | ~90% |
| Year 9 | ~80% | ~87% |

Running dimmed slows degradation because lower junction temperature is the primary factor in LED longevity.

### Driver Lifespan

The LED driver (power supply) is typically the first component to fail, not the LEDs themselves. Quality drivers from MeanWell (used in most reputable fixtures) are rated for 50,000-100,000 hours. External-mount drivers (outside the tent) last longer because they run cooler.
